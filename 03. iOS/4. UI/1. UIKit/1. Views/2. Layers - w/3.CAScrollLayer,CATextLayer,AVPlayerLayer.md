CAScrollLayer just allows to automatically scroll to a specified point, in case it has some sublayers. It is not even close to UIScrollView and does not provide scroll indicators, or even pan gesture tracking, etc.

scrollMode is a property which says which indicates the layer should be scrollable in which directions. The possible values are kCAScrollBoth, kCAScrollHorizontally, kCAScrollVertically and kCAScrollNone.

Scrolling is done using scrollToRect and scrollToPoint methods.

let l4: CAScrollLayer! = CAScrollLayer()
let l5: CALayer! = CALayer()
l4.scrollMode = kCAScrollBoth
l4.addSublayer(l5)
self.view.layer.addSublayer(l4)
l4.scrollToRect(CGRectMake(100.0, 100.0, 400.0, 400.0))

The practical use of CAScrollLayer seems to be limited. It can just do some elementary scrolling and even
UIScrollView itself  doesn’t use a CAScrollLayer to do its work. 

For scrolling a very large image, CATiledLayer is better.

CATextLayer provides quick rendering of plain or attributed text.  Does not have an assigned UIFont but instead works with CTFontRef or CGFontRef.

It has properties such as font, wrapped, alignmentMode (i.e. left, center, etc.) and truncationMode.

textLayer.font = CTFontCreateWithName(fontName, fontSize, nil)

AVPlayerLayer can be used to play videos. Its probably something to which even an AVPlayer directs its output.

It can be initialized with an AVPlayer object which in turn has the URL of the video to be played.
videoGravity property sets the resizing behavior of the video display. It defines how the video is displayed within a layer’s bounds rectangle. For example, AVLayerVideoGravityResizeAspect indicates that the player should preserve the video’s aspect ratio and fit the video within the layer’s bounds.

readyForDisplay property tells if the video is ready for display.

AVPlayer and its related classes have methods such as play, pause, rate (0.5x, 1x, 2x, etc.) and notifications such as AVPlayerItemDidPlayToEndTimeNotification to work withe the video being played.
